The workshop

Two literatures worth thousands of citations. Almost nothing in between.

Over three years, TabPFN, Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, KumoRFM and RelBench made a real advance: one model, many schemas, transfer where the field previously trained one model per dataset. But every one of them is trained purely observationally. They estimate what will happen. They cannot answer the question their users actually ask, which is what happens if I intervene.

Meanwhile the world-model literature - Dreamer, JEPA, learned simulators - has spent the same three years almost entirely on pixels and robots. Every architecture in wide use inherits a spatial inductive bias from images. Remove it and the open question becomes concrete: what replaces spatial structure when the state is a schema? A foreign-key graph, a process topology, a causal graph, an event grammar. The field has not decided.

WMSD is a day organised around one question: what would it take to build an action-conditioned, interventionally valid dynamics model over structured state? Databases you can roll forward under a policy. Factories and grids you can plan in. Fleets and processes whose future depends on what you do next.

Counts above are arXiv abstract searches, retrieved September 2026. The bridging papers between the two literatures - PLDM, RelBench, Relational Deep Learning, Richens & Everitt - sit under 100 citations each, against canon papers in the thousands.

Scope

What we want to see

01

Action-conditioned dynamics

Models of structured state that take an action and predict a consequence. Relational, tabular, event-stream and telemetry state. What the action space even is when the object is a database.

02

Latent state without a spatial prior

What replaces the inductive bias that pixels give you for free. Sufficiency, abstraction, identifiability, and what a schema contributes that a grid does not.

03

Learned simulators and digital twins

Industrial process, energy systems, supply chains, scientific measurement. Rollout stability, long-horizon error accumulation, and when a learned simulator beats the physics one.

04

Interventional evaluation

Benchmarks that can show a rollout is wrong. Counterfactual protocols for structured data. Contamination, leakage, and the strength of the baseline you were compared against.

05

Negative results

Where a structured-data world model does not beat a tuned baseline or a domain simulator, and why. We mean this: negative results are in scope and will be accepted.

06

Deployment in the wild

Domain shift, reliability, privacy, regulation, inference budgets. What breaks between the benchmark and the plant floor.

Out of scope, deliberately: embodied and visual world models, and clinical trial simulation. Both are well served elsewhere. Submissions are 4 pages, non-archival, double-blind on OpenReview, plus a short-paper track for late-breaking work. Novelty is not a requirement for acceptance. One author per submission serves on the program committee.

Format

A day built for exchange, not for talks.

Four invited talks, not twelve. Eight spotlights. Two poster sessions running 155 minutes, because posters are where the feedback and the collaborations actually happen. Invited talks take 26% of the clock; contributed work and interactive formats take 54%.

And no panel. Panels are where five senior people say "exciting times ahead" for 45 minutes.

The challenge

We will match you with a team.

One task, set by us and released with the call for papers. Given logged state, action and outcome data from a real structured system, predict the effect of an action nobody took. Scored on held-out interventions, not held-out time.

No interventional benchmark exists today for relational or telemetry data. Building one is the point.

The winning team presents on the day and takes a substantial cash prize, alongside an equally substantial award for best paper.

Support

Sponsors

Sponsorship funds the lunch, the best-paper and challenge prizes, student travel grants and the evening social. The ask is EUR 500-5,000, and every sponsor gets a logo on this site and a dedicated slide in the opening presentation.

Sponsors pay vendors and recipients directly. Catering is invoiced to the sponsor, travel grants are reimbursed by the sponsor, award money is paid by the sponsor to the winner. No funds pass through organizer accounts.

Sponsors have no role in peer review or programme selection, and sponsorship does not secure an industry spotlight slot - those are selected on technical content. One organizer is affiliated with SAP and two are at Forgis; all three organizations sponsor, and submissions from an organizer's institution are assigned to unaffiliated reviewers. We state this publicly and we mean it.

Where this comes from

A new workshop, not a new edition

WMSD is a first edition. It draws on the community assembled by Foundation Models for Structured Data at ICML 2025 and 2026, which showed there is a large audience for structured data as a first-class modality - and it asks a different question. FMSD is about models that predict. This one is about models that act.
